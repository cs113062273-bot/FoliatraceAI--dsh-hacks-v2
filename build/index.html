      04 · How it works

      Trust you can check.
      Limits you can see.

      01

      Verbatim NIH, verified by code.

      Every warning is a sentence from an NCCIH “Usefulness and Safety” fact sheet. A build script downloads each page and refuses to publish any quote that is not found word-for-word.

      02

      A name stays a hypothesis.

      “Ginseng” can be Asian, American or “Siberian” — different plants. FoliaTrace never guesses: it shows each candidate and which NIH statements would apply, until the label or pharmacist settles it.

      03

      Your health data stays with you.

      Medicines, conditions and label photos are processed in this browser. Text recognition and the research AI run on your device. Only label-search words go to NIH.

      Measured on real NIH-listed labels

      92%of graded botanical ingredients matched the NIH curator’s declared species from the name alone (787 labels, development sample)

      99.7%matched at genus level or better; the one “wrong genus” was a curator typo

      50%of botanical ingredients in those labels had no individual amount — hidden in proprietary blends

      Search-ranked samples from the NIH Dietary Supplement Label Database, not a random market sample. Method and per-herb results ship with the source (reports/label_matching_*.json).

      What FoliaTrace will not do

      • It never says “safe”. No NIH statement for a combination does not mean the combination is safe; NIH notes that “some herbs and medicines interact in harmful ways.”
      • It does not replace a pharmacist or give doses. It prepares the conversation.
      • It covers 25 commonly used herbs. Other botanicals are listed on your brief as “not covered” instead of being silently skipped.
      • It reads labels, not bottles. NIH notes that some products contain herbs not listed on the label.
      Where AI is used — and what has been measured

      Label photos: an LSTM text-recognition model (Tesseract) runs in the browser; tolerant name matching recovers common misreadings and the person reviews the text before it is used. Research ranking: a pinned cross-encoder reranks keyword candidates in a Web Worker. Safety statements are deterministic matches to verified NIH text — no generative model writes a warning.
